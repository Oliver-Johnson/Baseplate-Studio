Baseplates Bins Guide

01 Drawer & grid ▾

Usable height is drawer floor to the lowest thing above. Baseplate height is 4.25 mm unless your joint forced a solid floor — the baseplate tool passes the real figure across.
Only affects the material estimate, not the geometry. Thin parts — walls, floor, dividers, lip — print solid whatever you set; the feet are thick enough to be infilled, and they dominate. Assumes 2 perimeters and 4 solid top/bottom layers.

03 Drawer layout ▾

—
Drag across empty cells to place a bin. Click one to select it, drag it to move it, or pull a corner grip to resize. A ⚠ marks a bin that will not sit properly where it is — hover it for the reason. It stays put either way, in case you are about to place the thing that fixes it. Front of the drawer is at the bottom.
Keys: Del removes the selected bin, arrows nudge it, shift+arrows resize it, Ctrl+D duplicates, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z undo and redo.

04 New bins ▾

—
A spec foot is 4.75 mm and eats the interior — a 1-unit bin is left with 1.05 mm. Low profile shortens it to 2 mm for 3.80 mm instead, but it only seats on a low lip, never in a baseplate. Bottom-layer bins want Standard or Low lip.
Dividers are counts of walls, not compartments — 1 divider makes 2 compartments.
A scoop fills the front inside corner with a curve so you can sweep contents out — try 8 mm. The label shelf juts in from the top of the back wall with a 45° underside, so it prints without support; 12 mm suits a printed label. Both need their wall to exist.
Front is the front of the drawer, matching the map. Corner posts stay full height so the side walls keep their ends. Lowering any wall removes the stacking lip — nothing can sit on it.
Splits it into the fewest pieces that each fit, covering the same cells. They sit side by side on the baseplate, so nothing is lost but the wall between them — and if you want one continuous compartment, set the facing walls of the new bins to Open.
